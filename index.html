The first group is read through FRED, the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis database, which republishes each agency's figures unchanged. Economic figures are checked six times each weekday, after the agencies' release times, and once each weekend morning. Most are published monthly or quarterly, so most checks find nothing new. Headlines and crypto are refreshed every hour from about 6:45 am to 10:45 pm.

How the readings are computed

Changes, year-ago comparisons, spreads and ratios are calculated from the source series after each refresh. Where an indicator is derived, its formula is shown with it. Daily and weekly rates are charted as monthly averages, and the headline shows the latest daily or weekly figure.

The rank is the share of readings in the selected period at or below the latest one. The range runs from that period's low to its high. Economic history on this page starts in 2017, so the longest period is labelled "Max".

Correlations use monthly data since 2018. That window holds one pandemic and one inflation cycle, which dominate the result. A correlation shows how two series moved together. It does not show that one causes the other. The explanations of mechanism and effects describe standard economic relationships. They do not forecast.

Seasonal adjustment

Many series move with the calendar. Seasonal adjustment estimates that pattern and removes it, so one month can be compared with the next. Agencies revise the adjustment every year, which is one reason past months change.

  • SA: seasonally adjusted. Month-to-month changes are meaningful.
  • SAAR: seasonally adjusted and scaled to an annual rate. A quarter of 0.5% growth is reported as about 2%.
  • NSA: not adjusted. Compare with the same month a year earlier. Market rates are NSA because they have no seasonal pattern.

CPI and producer price inflation are 12-month changes computed from unadjusted indexes, which is how the Bureau of Labor Statistics states them. Core PCE uses the Bureau of Economic Analysis's adjusted index.

Market method

Index levels and share prices are daily closes. Returns are price returns and leave out dividends.

Index members and weights come from the daily holdings of the funds that track each index: SPY for the S&P 500, QQQ for the Nasdaq-100 and DIA for the Dow. The Nasdaq Composite holds more than 3,000 stocks, so its company view uses the Nasdaq-100.

Sectors follow the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) that S&P and MSCI maintain. S&P 500 members carry the sector and sub-industry listed in Wikipedia's table. Fifteen Nasdaq-100 companies outside the S&P 500 were classified by hand; they are listed in scripts/refresh_markets.py.

A sector's return is the weighted average of today's members, each weighted by its share of the index at the start of the period. Companies that left an index during the period are not counted, so sector returns over a year run higher than the index itself.

The VIX is Cboe's index of expected S&P 500 swings. The MOVE is ICE BofA's index of expected swings in Treasury yields.

Credit spreads and gaps

Credit spreads are ICE BofA option-adjusted spreads for the US corporate index, the US high yield index and each rating from AAA to CCC and lower. FRED serves a rolling three years of daily history for these series. The page keeps every day it has read, so its history grows beyond three years from here on.

A gap between two ratings is one spread minus the other on the same day. The one with the wider spread today is always listed first, so the gap reads as a positive number.

Crypto method

Prices, market value, trading volume, supply and the 24-hour, 7-day, 30-day and one-year changes come from CoinGecko, which averages prices across exchanges. The page asks CoinGecko for current figures when it opens and once a minute after that. If that fails, it shows the last saved snapshot and says so.

Price history is the daily close at midnight UTC: daily for the past 400 days and weekly before that. Year to date is measured from the December 31 close. The tab covers the largest tokens by market value. Dollar-pegged stablecoins and tokenised claims on other assets are left out.

Headlines

The ticker shows headlines from the outlets' public feeds, grouped as markets, economy, politics, technology, AI and crypto. Outlets take turns within each group so no single feed fills it. Each headline links to the publisher's article, and "List" shows them all at once. The time beside "Latest" is when the list was last refreshed.

How the data is checked

Every refresh rebuilds each indicator's full history and compares it with what is already on the page. An indicator that does not match keeps its last good reading, so a change at a source cannot put a wrong number here unnoticed.

Each data file is also checked before it is published: it must read correctly and hold a minimum number of indicators, companies, tokens or headlines. A file that fails is not published, and the previous one stays.

Known gaps

Federal statistical agencies did not collect some October 2025 data during the 2025 government shutdown. Those months appear as breaks in the affected charts, and year-over-year comparisons that need them are blank.

For the same reason, CPI inflation and the measures built on it have no October 2026 reading: there is no October 2025 figure to compare it with. These indicators move from September 2026 straight to November 2026, and are marked on the Indicators tab.

Retail sales are in current dollars. The shaded band on economic charts marks the 2020 recession as dated by the National Bureau of Economic Research.
