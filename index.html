Sources and method

Economic data

Each series comes from the agency that produces it. Labor and price data: Bureau of Labor Statistics. Treasury yields, federal debt, the deficit and the average rate on federal debt: US Treasury. Federal funds rate: Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Housing starts, permits, retail sales and business applications: Census Bureau. Mortgage rates: Freddie Mac. Consumer sentiment: University of Michigan.

GDP, the PCE price index, real consumer spending and the saving rate are Bureau of Economic Analysis series, and industrial production is a Federal Reserve Board series. These five are read through DBnomics, an open mirror of the official releases. Business AI adoption is the Ramp AI Index, read from Ramp's published page by hand. It does not refresh with the rest.

Market data

Index levels and share prices are daily closes from Yahoo Finance. Returns are price returns and exclude dividends.

Index members and weights come from the daily holdings of the funds that track each index: SPY for the S&P 500, QQQ for the Nasdaq-100 and DIA for the Dow. The Nasdaq Composite holds more than 3,000 stocks, so its company view uses the Nasdaq-100.

Sectors follow the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) that S&P and MSCI maintain. S&P 500 members carry the sector and sub-industry listed in Wikipedia's constituent table. Fifteen Nasdaq-100 companies outside the S&P 500 were classified by hand and are listed in scripts/refresh_markets.py.

A sector's return is the weighted average of today's members, each weighted by its share of the index at the start of the period. Companies that left an index during the period are not counted, so sector returns over a year run higher than the index itself.

The VIX is Cboe's index of expected S&P 500 volatility and the MOVE is ICE BofA's index of expected Treasury yield volatility. Both are daily closes from Yahoo Finance.

Credit spreads are ICE BofA option-adjusted spreads, read from FRED. FRED carries three years of history for these series, so ranges and ranks cover three years.

Headlines

The ticker shows headlines from the public feeds of Bloomberg, The Wall Street Journal and the Financial Times, grouped as markets, economy, politics, technology and AI. Each links to the publisher's article. The time beside "Latest" is when the list was last refreshed.

Seasonal adjustment

Many series move with the calendar. Seasonal adjustment estimates that pattern and removes it so one month can be compared with the next. Agencies revise the adjustment every year, which is one reason past months change.

  • SA: seasonally adjusted. Month-to-month changes are meaningful.
  • SAAR: seasonally adjusted and scaled to an annual rate. A quarter of 0.5% growth is reported as about 2%.
  • NSA: not adjusted. Compare with the same month a year earlier. Market rates are NSA because they have no seasonal pattern.

CPI and producer price inflation are 12-month changes computed from unadjusted indexes, which is how the Bureau of Labor Statistics states them. Core PCE uses the Bureau of Economic Analysis's adjusted index.

How the readings are computed

Changes, year-ago comparisons, spreads and ratios are calculated from the source series after each refresh. Where an indicator is derived, its formula is shown with it. Daily and weekly rates are charted as monthly averages, and the headline shows the latest daily or weekly figure. The rank is the share of readings in the selected period at or below the latest one, and the range runs from that period's low to its high.

Correlations use monthly data since 2018. That window holds one pandemic and one inflation cycle, which dominate the result. A correlation shows how two series moved together. It does not show that one causes the other.

The explanations of mechanism and effects describe standard economic relationships. They do not forecast.

Known gaps

Federal statistical agencies did not collect some October 2025 data during the lapse in appropriations. Those months appear as breaks in the affected charts, and year-over-year comparisons that need them are blank. Retail sales are in current dollars. The shaded band on economic charts marks the 2020 recession as dated by the National Bureau of Economic Research.
